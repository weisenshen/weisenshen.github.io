Education & Open Learning

My educational work trains students to understand Earth systems through field observation, data, computation, instrumentation, and public relevance. Beginning in Fall 2026, I am building a connected set of courses, lab-based projects, and open educational resources that support classroom teaching, research training, and broader public learning.

Vision

Educational Vision

Open by design

Courses should produce reusable, citable Earth-science materials: notes, notebooks, datasets, workflows, and examples that outlive a single semester.

Courses as living resources

A course is not a fixed packet of lectures. It is an evolving educational resource that improves as students, data, instruments, and public questions change.

Access to modern Earth science

Students should learn with the tools that modern Earth scientists actually use: sensors, maps, code, models, uncertainty, and public-facing products.

Courses

Courses and Course Development

GEO 542: Machine Learning & Inverse Theory in Earth Sciences

Quantitative inference for Earth systems: inverse problems, model uncertainty, machine learning, and the discipline of asking data what it can actually tell us.

ESS 542: Tectonic Environments

A graduate-level view of tectonic settings, lithospheric structure, and the physical processes that shape continents, margins, basins, and mountain belts.

VIP / ARGOS Project Course

A project-based course where students build instruments, maps, datasets, and public Earth-system products as part of a research and education ecosystem.

Future: Surface Processes & Geohazards

A course connecting landscapes, water, sediment, hazards, field observation, and decision-making in a changing environment.

Future: Earth Science, Data, Policy & Society

Earth science does not stop at the edge of a map. This course will connect data, natural resources, risk, finance, policy, and civic life.

ARGOS

Instrumentation & Mapping

ARGOS is the lab and project environment where students learn by building. The goal is a renovated space that supports instrumentation, field systems, mapping, fabrication, and student-built research and teaching tools.

Public

New York Earth Systems

I want part of this educational ecosystem to serve New York directly: geology and Earth processes of the state, Long Island groundwater, environmental and resource maps, ZIP-code environmental rankings, municipal reports, and geophysical monitoring networks.

These products should be useful beyond the classroom. If students build maps and tools that local communities can read, critique, and use, the course has done more than assign a grade. It has entered public life.

Students

Student Projects

Students are not just users of course materials. They are builders and contributors. Their projects should connect courses, ARGOS, open resources, and public-facing Earth systems products.

The best student work should become part of the educational infrastructure for the next group: better maps, better notebooks, better instruments, better explanations, better questions.
