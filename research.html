Methods 2011 - present

Seismic Imaging and Bayesian Inversion

I develop and apply Bayesian joint inversion methods that combine multiple seismic observables: surface wave dispersion, receiver functions, and Rayleigh wave ellipticity. The goal is not just a model, but a model with honest uncertainty.

Reference seismic models for the United States and China from this work are used by the community in studies of lithospheric dynamics, resource exploration, and seismic hazard.

A newer direction uses seismic models to infer what the crust is made of and how hot it is, connecting seismic observables to crustal composition, thermal structure, and rheology.

From velocity to composition and temperature

Work with Suyu Sui develops ways to translate seismic properties into constraints on crustal composition and thermal state, including applications to Antarctica and the conterminous United States.

Sui et al., 2022 · Sui et al., 2025

Combining more observables

New methods led by Hanxiao Wu incorporate additional techniques into joint inversion workflows, including H-k stacking and Monte Carlo inversion of multiple seismic observables.

Antarctica 2018 - present

Polar Geophysics

My field program deploys seismic and magnetotelluric instruments across Antarctica to characterize the solid Earth beneath the ice: bedrock geology, crustal structure, geothermal heat flux, and subglacial hydrology.

South Pole and East Antarctica

A large-scale seismic array near the South Pole has produced new constraints on crustal structure and sub-ice geology in East Antarctica, including evidence for a large-scale subglacial aquifer system with implications for ice-sheet dynamics and sea level.

Manuscript in preparation · NSF CAREER

Geothermal Heat Flux

I produced the first seismology-derived geothermal heat flux map of Antarctica, now used as a community reference dataset in ice-sheet modeling and basal melt rate estimation.

Shen et al., 2020 · Geophysical Research Letters

Ross Region and Dry Valleys

Planned projects in the Ross region and McMurdo Dry Valleys would combine seismic methods, ocean acoustic sensing, and co-located geophysical and geochemical characterization to study ice-ocean-solid Earth interaction and shallow subsurface structure.

Observatory 2025 - present

Union Glacier Observational Platform

My group is developing a permanent geophysical observational platform at Union Glacier in the Ellsworth Mountains, West Antarctica, with support from the U.S. Department of Defense. The platform hosts solid Earth instruments alongside space weather sensors, including magnetometers and GNSS scintillation monitors.

Union Glacier sits within the auroral oval and provides observations from a chronically under-monitored region of the Southern Hemisphere, where simultaneous space weather data from Arctic conjugate points is scientifically valuable.

Global Emerging

Global Water Systems

Sub-ice geological surveys in Antarctica suggest that subglacial groundwater plays a larger role in ice-sheet dynamics than previously recognized. This motivates a broader interest in using solid Earth geophysical methods - seismic velocity, geodetic loading signals, and electromagnetic sounding - to constrain global groundwater storage.

Climate Emerging

Climate Science Connections

I am not a climate scientist. My work helps the climate research community better understand the physical conditions and processes that are critical to their work.

The physical boundary conditions that govern ice-sheet behavior - bedrock topography, sub-ice geology, geothermal heat flux, and subglacial hydrology - are also the variables that determine the feasibility of proposed climate interventions targeting glacial systems. My field data contributes from an empirical standpoint: characterize what the solid Earth actually looks like, and be honest about what remains unknown.
